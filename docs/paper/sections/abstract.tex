An edge node that offloads sensor windows to a cloud model must decide what to send: raw samples,
a compressed waveform, engineered features or a learned latent. We certify that choice. Conformal
risk control on the event that the cloud's decision would differ from its decision on the raw
window yields a per-window \emph{safe set} of representations. When calibration and test windows
are exchangeable, the expected rate, per offered window, of decisions that are both certified and
in disagreement with the raw-data decision is at most $\alpha$ for \emph{any} selector that chooses
inside the set, so the cost optimiser can be replaced without a new proof. We evaluate on
bearing vibration, human activity, speech and sleep EEG under leave-units-out splits, against
seven pre-registered hypotheses. The window-level certificate's mean risk lands near $\alpha$; its
pre-registered test is met only on one dataset and is not significant there. A union-bound cascade
of a fixed and a per-window set, added after the first results and therefore exploratory, keeps
mean risk below $\alpha$ on every dataset rerun with the final code. Per-window adaptive selection
fails its pre-registered value test on every dataset. On three of four, an uncertified fixed
choice is cheaper, because when the certificate refuses the cheap latent the next certified payload
is too large for a slow link; on the fourth, adaptive choice is cheaper but misses the SLO-reduction
target. A mixed menu clears the pre-registered 10\,\% margin over the best single representation
family on two datasets. The simulator
matches a real-socket runtime within 10\,\% at the median on three of four rerun datasets and in a
Docker testbed. We report the negative results as such and release the pipeline.
