\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Unless stated otherwise, results are for the \texttt{metered\_cellular} preset, means over folds,
with laptop timings and modelled energy. Variants and policies marked \expl{} are exploratory.

\subsection{The menu}
\label{sec:res-menu}

(\rep{r0}--\rep{r2b}) are kilobytes and agree with $\fraw$ on nearly every window. Of the compact

\paragraph{V0 lands near $\alpha$.} This is what an expected-risk bound predicts when the bound is
tight. H2a is met only on Sleep-EDF, and Holm does not reject there (adjusted $p = 1.00$). On
Paderborn the mean is below $\alpha$ at every level but the bearing-block CI upper bound (0.110)
fails the $\alpha+0.01$ criterion at $\alpha = 0.1$; on Speech Commands the point risk is just above $\alpha$ at every level; on HAR
it is above $\alpha$ at $\alpha = 0.1$. The pre-registered one-sided test (mean $< \alpha$) cannot
reject by design for a method whose expected risk sits at $\alpha$, so H2a cannot be
\emph{supported} under the registered rule even when the bound holds. H2b fails everywhere: V1 is
within $\alpha$ in fewer than 90\,\% of folds at some level on every dataset.

% src: E2 "Exchangeability control" bullet of each report
\paragraph{Exchangeability control.} Calibrated on a random half of the calibration windows and
tested on the other half (20 halvings), V0's risk is within 0.004 of $\alpha$ on every dataset
(Paderborn: 0.020, 0.050, 0.099 at $\alpha = 0.02, 0.05, 0.1$; HAR: 0.018, 0.046, 0.098). Where
the premise holds by construction, the bound is tight. The deviations on test units are consistent
with unit shift; with five folds they are also within sampling noise.

% src: E2 "H2a non-inferiority" bullet of each report; updates.md §7, fix 4
\paragraph{Non-inferiority\expl.} A one-sided test of mean V0 risk $< \alpha + 0.01$, with the
margin fixed before it was computed and outside the Holm family, rejects on Paderborn (maximum $p$
over $\alpha$ = 0.032) and Speech Commands (0.040), and does not reject on HAR (0.338) or Sleep-EDF
(0.084). This is consistent with the bound; it is not stronger evidence for it, and the fold
p-values are optimistic.

% src: E2 tables; updates.md §6 "What it means" (29 of 30 folds)
\paragraph{The cascade and the static set\expl.} The cascade's mean risk is below $\alpha$ at every
level on every rerun dataset, 0.019--0.027 at $\alpha = 0.05$; descriptively, 29 of 30 folds over
the four datasets are within $\alpha$ at $\alpha = 0.05$. The margin is the slack of the
$\alpha/2 + \alpha/2$ split; the union bound itself only gives an expectation $\le\alpha$. The
static set is within $\alpha$ in every fold at every level on all four datasets. Its cheapest
certified payload at $\alpha = 0.05$ is 0.2\,\% of raw on Paderborn (\rep{r6}), 12\,\% on Speech
Commands, 24\,\% on Sleep-EDF and 84\,\% on HAR: a small fraction of raw only where the menu has a
compact representation close to $\fraw$.

\paragraph{Group CRC\expl.} With one sample per unit, the method needs 19 calibration units at
$\alpha = 0.05$ and 9 at $\alpha = 0.1$. Only Speech Commands has enough at every level; there the
mean over units sits on $\alpha$ (0.0496 at $\alpha = 0.05$, 0.101 at $\alpha = 0.1$). Sleep-EDF has enough only at
$\alpha = 0.1$ (0.032). Paderborn and HAR have too few at every level, and group CRC returns raw
only: correct, but it saves nothing.

\paragraph{H1 and H3 fail on every dataset.} One compact representation is the oracle's main
choice in nearly every cell (on Paderborn \rep{r6}, 99.5\,\% of cells). On HAR, Kai is 26.4\,\%
cheaper than the best single representation, meeting H3(a), but its SLO reduction of 1.7\,\% misses
H3(b). On Paderborn, Speech Commands and Sleep-EDF Kai costs more than B4.

% src: results.md §E3 policy table and bandwidth breakdown; updates.md §6 "What it means"
\paragraph{Why.} On Paderborn (mean over $\alpha$ and cells), Kai V0 costs 3.1 times B4
($9.46\times10^7$ against $3.04\times10^7$ per fold), the cascade 2.0 times and the static set 1.4
times. Kai holds back 28.6\,\% of windows: their safe set excludes \rep{r6}, the next certified
options are kilobyte-sized wavelets or raw, these do not reach the cloud before the deadline, and
each held-back window is priced as an SLO miss (violation rate 0.30 against 0.19 for B4). The
effect is a slow-link effect: on static links Kai defers 47.8\,\% of windows at 64~kbit/s and
0.07\,\% at 5~Mbit/s. B4 sends \rep{r6} on those windows anyway, and 22.5\,\% of its frames fall
outside the safe set. B4 screens representations with a marginal CRC-style check on calibration
data, but applied to every representation, keeping the cheapest that passes, the check bounds
nothing (multiple testing): B4 is cheaper and \emph{uncertified}. The certified fixed-choice
comparator is the static set, which has the fewest SLO misses of all certified policies (0.14).

% src: results.md §E3 kai_s1_auto bullet and gate AUCs; results.md §E5 no_predictor row
\paragraph{The predictor has little signal on Paderborn.} Its unit-grouped CV AUC on
\texttt{ptrain} is 0.46--0.57 across folds, and the ablation without a predictor costs 0.33 of
Kai's total. A gate that falls back to the static set below an AUC of 0.55\expl{} picks the cheaper
branch on every dataset but three Paderborn folds just above the cut. It does not rescue H3:
where V0 lost to B4, both branches lose.

% src: results.md §E3 "other price presets" table
\paragraph{Other prices\expl.} Pricing energy, edge CPU time or lateness instead of bytes does not
change the answer on Paderborn: Kai V0 and the cascade cost more than B4 under all four presets.

\subsection{Ablations: encode cost and heterogeneity}
\label{sec:res-ablation}

% src: E5 H4 / H5 bullets of each report; results.md §E5 "× 8"
\paragraph{H4 fails everywhere.} Ignoring encode cost changes the main choice in 0.2\,\% of
high-load cells on Paderborn, 0.0\,\% on HAR, 2.9\,\% on Speech Commands and 2.4\,\% on Sleep-EDF,
against a threshold of 15\,\%. With edge CPU times multiplied by eight, a modelled slower edge, it
is 0.8\,\% on Paderborn. Encoders take below a millisecond on the laptop, so load rarely changes
the choice. Only a run on a Pi-class edge can test H4 as intended.

\paragraph{H5 depends on the menu.} The full menu's cost reduction against the best single-family
controller is $+5.8\,\%$ on Paderborn, $+10.9\,\%$ on HAR (met, Holm-adjusted $p = 1.00$),
$+5.0\,\%$ on Speech Commands and $+21.0\,\%$ on Sleep-EDF (supported, CI [$+16.3$, $+23.3$]).
With the exploratory log-mel family on Speech Commands\expl{} it is $+15.9\,\%$ (CI [$+15.4$,
$+16.9$]), passing the threshold and the Holm test; it is a second family on the same speakers,
built after seeing the first run, and not confirmatory. A mixed menu pays where it holds a small
representation close to the raw decision.

\paragraph{H6: closed-form boundaries.} H6 fails on every dataset in its pre-registered run. The
closed form predicts few boundaries, while the measured argmin changes many times among compact
representations whose latencies differ by less than the measurement noise; on Paderborn 5 of 16
measured changes matched a predicted boundary, the worst with a relative error of 8716\,\%. A later
re-measurement, triggered by an exploratory fix, met the threshold on Speech Commands; a verdict
that changes between measurements of the same thing is not counted as met, and the verdict of
record stays the pre-registered run. Under a stricter rule\expl{} that counts a measured change
only when the latency gap exceeds twice the combined MAD at both grid points, no change survives on
any dataset. That rule is biased towards ``tied'', so this does not show that H6 cannot be tested;
a rule based on the standard error of the median, or a finer bandwidth grid, was not tried.

\subsection{Shift and drift}
\label{sec:res-drift}

% src: results.md §E6
On Paderborn at $\alpha = 0.05$, holding bearings out (L1), the safe-set risk with the static
threshold is 0.052; also holding one operating condition out (L2), it is 0.129. The certificate
does not survive a condition shift. The realised risk of the greedy policy is far lower (0.007 and
0.010), and audit-in-the-loop ACI keeps it at 0.007 and 0.009, sending 187~B and 376~B per window
against 206~B and 455~B for the static threshold (audit copies: 142--147~B per window). ACI tracks
realised risk and has no finite-sample guarantee. The probe-feature monitor fires on 97\,\% of test
windows under L1 and 89\,\% under L2: it detects that test bearings differ from calibration
bearings even without a condition shift. As a safe-mode trigger it would switch every new machine
to raw.

\subsection{Privacy\expl}
\label{sec:res-privacy}

% src: results.md §E10
No hypothesis was registered for this probe. A logistic-regression attacker on what the cloud
decodes from \rep{r6} re-identifies a seen bearing with accuracy 0.589 (chance 0.18 on average) and
recognises the operating condition of an unseen machine with 0.338 (chance 0.25). A compact payload
hides the waveform, not the machine's identity or operating point.

\subsection{Icentia11k}
\label{sec:res-icentia}

% src: results-icentia.md (all numbers in this subsection)
Icentia11k~\cite{tan2019icentia} is single-lead ambulatory ECG at 250\,Hz: the first segment of 980
patients, cut into 16.4\,s windows labelled V, S or N by the beats they hold. The patient is the
unit: five leave-patients-out folds with 194 calibration patients each, enough for group CRC at
every $\alpha$. It ran after every rule of ADR~0022 was fixed, and from E3 on with the corrected
simulator (ADR~0023), so its E3 and E5 numbers are not comparable with the earlier-simulator
numbers above. Its data was touched by the October dataset screening, so it is the
closest-to-unseen test available, not a confirmatory one. $\fraw$ (the waveform network, chosen by
inner cross-validation as in Section~\ref{sec:protocol}) reaches macro-F1 0.641 on unseen patients
(majority baseline 0.257).

\emph{Certificate.} V0's mean safe-set risk over folds is 0.0212, 0.0477 and 0.0934 at
$\alpha = 0.02, 0.05, 0.1$; each block-bootstrap CI contains $\alpha$. H2a is not supported: as on
every dataset, its one-sided test cannot reject when the risk lands at $\alpha$. The exploratory non-inferiority test (margin 0.01) rejects (maximum
$p = 0.041$). The exchangeability control lands at 0.020, 0.050 and 0.101. V1 has 4 of 5 folds
within $\alpha$, so H2b fails. Group CRC\expl{} averages 0.0172, 0.0458 and 0.0922 over test
patients; the cascade\expl{} has 0.0132, 0.0279 and 0.051 per fold and lies within $\alpha$ in all
five folds.

\emph{Cost.} The oracle picks \rep{r6} in every cell, so H1 fails (one representation; best-single
costs 120\,\% more than the oracle). H3 fails: Kai (S1, V0) costs 145\,\% more than best-single
(95\,\% CI of the reduction $[-180\,\%, -130\,\%]$), and on bursty traces it has 77\,\% more SLO
violations than the context-blind baseline. In the exploratory static-link breakdown (nodes with
fewer than 32 sensors), Kai is cheaper than best-single from 1\,Mb/s up and loses below it, where it defers 24--44\,\% of windows
because nothing in their safe set fits the link. Best-single defers none and is uncertified. H5 is
supported: the full menu costs 18.9\,\% less than the best single family (CI $[17.1\,\%, 21.2\,\%]$,
Holm $p = 0.006$; exploratory Nadeau--Bengio Holm $p = 0.028$). H4 fails (0\,\% of high-load
cells change choice).

\emph{Timing.} H6 fails: 1 of 15 measured argmin changes matches one of the 2 predicted boundaries,
with relative error 811\,\%. Under the MAD rule\expl{} (ADR~0022) 0 of 1 change matches, and
under the median-SE rule\expl{} (ADR~0024) 0 of 3. Inside the waveform family\expl, 2 of 4
predicted boundaries match, worst error 62\,\%. H7 meets its threshold: worst p50 gap 2.5\,\%,
representation agreement 100\,\%, over loopback on the same laptop. In the privacy
probe\expl{}, an attacker on \rep{r6} re-identifies a seen patient with accuracy 0.031 (chance
0.010 on average), against 0.751 on the raw window's band-energy shape.
